# 04.2 — The first experiment: rotation sweeps around the supervised optimum

This note builds on note 04.1. It defines the first experiment. Part 1 defines the search space of the experiment. Part 2 defines the experiment itself, which is the one implemented by the S8 and S9 stages of the `nn/` folder (`nn/s8_supervised_rotation.py`, `nn/s9_rotation_sweep.py`, and the variants `s8_2_…` and `s9_2_…`).

---

# Part 1. The search space

The experiment compares pairs of **(simplicity metric, inference method)**. The search space therefore has two blocks.

## 1.1 Block 1 — the metric

The simplicity metric `l = L(R)`: any simplicity measure that is computed from the H matrix (note 04.1, Parts 1 and 2). Metrics differ in which block of the H matrix they look at (UU, UX, or both), in what they quantify (sparsity, complexity, nonlinearity, …) and in how the expectation is taken (integral or average, note 04.1 section 2.3). The metric library is in [`docs/01_metrics/01_hessian_mean_squared_curvature_energy.md`](../01_metrics/01_hessian_mean_squared_curvature_energy.md).

## 1.2 Block 2 — the inference method

The inference method `Inf`: how to obtain the metric when only `R`, `Z`, `X`, `Y` and `d` are available, so that the result is **accurate**, i.e. close to the metric computed from the analytic function. The branches are those of note 04.1, Part 3 (infer the H matrix first or `l` directly; whether and how `d'` is used; whether an analytic form is fitted first; integrate over `Z` or average over subjects; and so on).

# Part 2. The current experiment

## 2.1 The rotations that are tested

The experiment does not search for `R`. It evaluates the metric on a fixed, known set of rotations that surround the best one.

**Step 1 — the supervised optimum `R*` (stage S8).** Use `T` as supervision to obtain the best rotation `R*`: the orthogonal rotation of `Z` that best matches the standardised `T` (an orthogonal Procrustes fit on the training subjects). `U = R* Z` is then in one-to-one correspondence with `T`. In the variant S9 the rotation is fitted on the raw `Z`. In the variant S9_2 it is fitted on `Z` after z-scoring each dimension over all subjects.

**Step 2 — rotating around `R*` (stage S9).** Starting from `R*`, rotate by a set of angles within chosen sets of axes. The sets of axes are all subsets of the `T`-aligned axes of size 2 (pairs), 3 (triples), 4 (quadruples), and so on up to all axes. For each subset and each angle in

```
0°, 15°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 75°, 90°
```

a new rotation `R` is built. For a subset of axes `(a_1, …, a_k)` the same angle is applied to the ordered adjacent planes `(a_1, a_2), (a_2, a_3), …, (a_{k−1}, a_k)`, giving the matrix `G`. The new rotation is

```
R = G · R*
```

For pairs this is an ordinary plane rotation. For larger subsets it is one explicitly defined ordered sweep, not a unique `k`-dimensional angle.

**Every angle of every axis subset gives one rotation `R`.** The unrotated `R*` is also included as a baseline. The rotations at 0° and at 90° are the same as `R*` up to signs and a permutation of the axes, so they keep the one-to-one correspondence with `T`. The more the angle moves away from 0° and 90°, the more the axes are mixed.

## 2.2 The two evaluations

For a metric `l` and an inference method `Inf`, two quantities are computed for **every rotation**:

- the **analytic** metric `l_analytic(R)`, computed from the generating function with the true `T`. For a rotation `R = G R*`, the corresponding rotation of the standardised `T` is `G`;
- the **inferred** metric `l_inferred(R)`, computed with `Inf` from `R`, `Z`, `X`, `Y` and `d` only.

### Evaluation 1 — correlation between the analytic and the inferred metric

Do the analytic metric and the inferred metric agree? **Each rotation is one sample point.** For a database (a test bed and a trained NP), the correlation between `l_analytic` and `l_inferred` over all sample points is computed. Pearson and Spearman are both used. A rotation that appears several times is counted once: the 0° entries of the sweeps are copies of `R*`, so the sample points are `R*` plus every rotation with an angle larger than 0°. The correlation along one curve (the 7 angles of one axis subset) can be added as a second view.

This evaluation measures Gap 2 of note 03: is the inferred metric a faithful reconstruction of the analytic one?

### Evaluation 2 — where the minimum is along the angle

For a given metric `l` and inference method `Inf`, look at each sweep (one axis subset, all angles). The question is whether the minimum of the metric is at the rotation that has the one-to-one correspondence, i.e. at no rotation. This is checked for **both** the analytic and the inferred metric.

- **Perfect pass.** In all the sweeps of the database, the minimum is at the unrotated position: at 0° or 90°.
- **Marginal pass (kept, but only just).** In all the sweeps of the database, the minimum is at the unrotated position or at a deviation of one step (15°) from it: at 0°, 15°, 75° or 90°.

Conventions:

- Ties are counted when the values are equal within a tiny relative tolerance. When the minimum is tied between 0° and 90°, it is reported as 0°.
- A curve that is constant or undefined (for example an analytic UU-only metric of a function that has no curvature in `U`) does not count as a pass.
- A metric passes a database only when it passes in **every** sweep of that database, for the analytic and for the inferred values.

This evaluation is a local test of the angle around `R*`. It measures Gap 1 (does the metric prefer the one-to-one rotation, analytic values) and Gap 2 (does the inferred metric agree with it) near the optimum. It is not a search over all rotations. A pass here does not by itself prove that a global search will find `R*`; that is Gap 3 of note 03.

## 2.3 Summary

| Item | Definition |
|---|---|
| Object compared | a pair `(l, Inf)` |
| Rotations | `R*` and, for every axis subset (size 2 up to all axes) and angle 0°, 15°, …, 90°, `R = G R*` |
| Variants | S9: fit on raw `Z`; S9_2: fit on z-scored `Z` |
| Evaluation 1 | correlation of `l_analytic` and `l_inferred`, one sample per rotation |
| Evaluation 2 | angle of the minimum for analytic and inferred values: perfect = 0° or 90° in all sweeps; marginal = 0°, 15°, 75° or 90° in all sweeps |
